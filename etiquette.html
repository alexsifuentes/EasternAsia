Eastern Asia Tips and Info

Food Etiquette

  • Japan and South Korea uses chopsticks as a way to eat food commonly.
  • What can be a simple error to us foreigners can mean something completely different for locals.
  • In Japan, they will say いただきます (itadakimasu) and ごちそさまでした (Gochisosama deshita) before and after a meal respectively. It is to show respect and be thankful for everyone that made the meal they are eating possible.
  • In South Korea, there are different food etiquettes than Japan, such as eating food on the same pace as others on your table that you are dining with.
  • South Korea also has a saying before taking the first bite. 잘 먹겠습니다 (al meokgesseumnida) means that you will eat well.
  • These are a few food etiquette manners that you'll have to know, there are many out there that you will learn either by researching it online or having locals teach you it!

Common Phrases

  • Understanding just the basics of a language can help you for ther long term when trying to communicate with locals.
  • Japan is a very polite and respect-driven society, so it is important to show respect and kindness to locals.
  • In Japan, there are a variety of different ways to say a phrase to another person, depending on age, rank, status, and other factors. Here are some examples:
    • ありがとございます (arigatou gozaimasu) is a very polite way to say thank you, while ありがとう (arigatou) is more casual to friends and family.
    • わかりません (wakarimasen) is a polite way to say I don't know. Be careful when saying 知られ (shirane), this is a rude way for saying I don't know!
  • In South Korea, there are also phrases that you might say to be polite and respectful.
    • 감사합니다 (kamsahamnida) is a polite way to say thank you.
    • 안녕하세요 (annyeonghaseyo) means hello, which you might say when you are meeting with someone.
